Safety and wellbeing

Hearth is meant to be a gentle place to spend some time. Here's how it tries to stay one, and where to find a real person when you need one.

Hearth characters are AI. What they say is made up.

If you might be in danger right now

If you're thinking about suicide, or worried you might hurt yourself, please reach out to a person now. These services are free and confidential, and they're there any time of day.

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline Call or text 988 in the US. Call 988 Text 988
  • Crisis Text Line Text HOME to 741741 in the US. Text HOME
  • Outside the US Find a free helpline in your country at findahelpline.com. findahelpline.com

If you're in immediate danger, call your local emergency number (911 in the US).

Hearth characters are AI

The residents of Lantern Bay are characters written for Hearth. Their words come from a language model running on your device. They can be wrong, forget things, or say something odd, and they can't stand in for a doctor, therapist, lawyer or financial advisor.

Every chat shows a quiet reminder that the characters are AI. In chats and group scenes, the residents are also written to tell you they're AI characters in Hearth if you sincerely ask whether they're real. They can get this wrong, so trust the reminder on screen.

When someone may be in danger

Hearth keeps a simple watch for signs that someone may be in danger. Here is what it does, and what it can't do.

  • It notices worrying words. The app looks for certain phrases that suggest someone may be thinking about self-harm or is in danger, like saying they want to end their life. It's a fixed list, so it can miss the same thing said another way. The check runs on your device, and nothing is reported anywhere.
  • It shows help without taking over. When it notices something in a chat or group scene, the crisis lines above appear right there. It doesn't lock you out or end the conversation. Get support, in any chat or scene menu, brings the lines back whenever you want them.
  • The reply starts with care. When the words sound serious, the iPhone app always starts the resident's reply with a fixed, caring line that points you to 988 (call or text, in the US). Then the resident goes on in their own words, told to step out of the story and stay with you. The resident is AI and may not get that part right, which is why the app shows the crisis lines itself.
  • On the web, a fixed reply. In the web app, a message that sounds serious gets a fixed, caring reply that lists the crisis lines, and it never goes to the AI model.
  • Replies stay as text. A reply to a worrying message never comes as a voice note or a picture.
  • It errs on the side of care. Even a passing mention, like "I would never hurt myself", brings up the resources softly, just in case.

This safety net can miss things, and it can misread a joke. It's no substitute for a person.

Because nothing leaves your device, Hearth can't call or alert anyone for you. If you're in danger, please contact a crisis line or emergency services yourself, or ask someone near you for help.

Made for adults

Hearth is for people 18 and older, and asks you to confirm your age the first time you open it. The built-in residents are friends and neighbors, and none of them are written as romantic partners.

Characters you create or import are yours to shape, and the same safety net applies to every chat.

How Hearth tries to keep things healthy

  • No streaks. No points, badges or daily rewards for coming back.
  • No notifications. Hearth never pings you to return.
  • No guilt. Residents are written never to pressure you to stay or make you feel bad for leaving.
  • Your people come first. Residents are written to be glad when you mention friends, family or plans.
  • Honesty. Every chat carries the AI reminder, and residents are written to answer truthfully about being AI.

No streaks, no notifications and the AI reminder are built into the app. The rest is how the residents are written. They're AI, so they can still get it wrong. If one does, please tell us.

A few things that can help

  • Use Hearth as one part of your day, alongside people, time outside, rest and the things you enjoy.
  • If you notice you're skipping things you care about to stay in the app, it may be a good moment for a break, or to talk with someone you trust.
  • If you'd like more company in person, libraries, community centers, clubs, faith groups and volunteering are good places to meet people at your own pace.
  • In many US states, warmlines offer free peer support by phone for hard days that aren't emergencies.

Reporting a problem

If a resident says something hurtful or unsafe, please tell us by opening an issue at github.com/aarushkandukoori/hearth/issues, so the characters and the safety net can get better. Issues are public, so describe what happened without sharing personal details.
